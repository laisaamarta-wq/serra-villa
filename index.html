The villa

Two long levels of travertine, set into the ridge so the hill keeps its shape.

Serra follows the slope instead of fighting it. The living floor opens onto a 28-metre pool that ends at the edge of the cliff; the bedrooms sit above under a slatted oak pergola.

Every room faces the bay. Pines, limestone and wild rosemary come right up to the walls.

Architecture

Every house begins as a line

Serra was drawn for one ridge above the sea, and for nothing else. The view was designed first; the rooms were arranged behind it.

The plan is two long bars stepped into the hillside. The lower one holds the living floor and the pool, the upper one the master suite under a pergola of oak slats. One thin roof slab carries the line of the ridge out toward the water.

          Life at Serra

          Days that move with the shade

          Outdoors

          The house has two outdoor rooms: the pool terrace on the living floor, and the pergola terrace above it, where the slatted oak draws moving stripes of shade from noon until sunset and the long table is laid for dinner.

          Privacy

          Serra sits at the end of a private road. The hill rises behind it and the pines close in on both sides, so the only open side is the one that faces the water.

          Rest

          Every suite faces the bay. In the master suite the bed looks straight out over the water, and the stone bath is set where the pines frame the view.

          The garden

          The old olive trees stayed where they were during construction. Limestone steps laid by hand lead between them down the slope.
